### Session 2.5a – Rumors, Tall-Tales, There Must Be a Strahd of Truth...

**Players**: Lyasra  
**Summary:**

  

  - Lyasra, still orienting herself to Barovia and its people, seeks out Ismark near the western ramparts where villagers are constructing defenses — barricades, caltrops, and trenches
  - She opens by asking Ismark about the mob's chanting from the previous session. He explains that the slogans ("red means dead," "trade the girl") stem from an old Barovian superstition: *"a girl of red brings monsters and dread."* The target is his sister **Ireena**
  - Ismark defends his people with evident pain — he names specific villagers and their sacrifices: Bildrath bankrupting himself to supply the defenses, a guard named Kreza who hasn't slept in a week, Mary grieving Gertruda's disappearance, Alenka who lost her sister and husband in last night's siege
  - Lyasra pushes back on mob logic without sympathy for superstition but without cruelty toward the villagers. Ismark reads her correctly — a fighter with history, not a fresh adventurer
  - Lyasra briefly lets her guard down, acknowledging she doesn't know why she's in Barovia, and quietly reveals her past: brigand, enforcer, butcher. Now a penitent. Ismark receives this without judgment
  - Ismark briefs Lyasra on the **Svalich Woods**: dangerous day and night, worse after dark; inhabited by a **druidic cult**, a **werewolf pack**, and increasing numbers of **undead** congregating at the tree line; waves of undead hit the village each night
  - Ismark makes a direct request: when night falls, hold the **eastern defenses** — they were hit hardest in the previous siege and need the morale boost
  - Lyasra stops short of promising success but offers something more personal: *"If the undead reach you, it will be over my ruined corpse."* She displays her massive spiked shield — an unmovable bulwark, clearly veteran equipment
  - Ismark closes the scene with quiet solidarity: *"My home could use greater men than me. But I am all they have."*

  

**A laborer behaved suspiciously** during Lyasra's conversation with Ismark — quickening his pace and moving out of earshot when Lyasra mentioned knocking sense into the mob. Lyasra tracked his scent. Ismark did not notice. **This thread is unresolved.**

  

**Key revelations & consequences:**

  

  - The mob's anti-Ireena sentiment is confirmed as rooted in old superstition, not specific grievance — Ismark is aware of it now thanks to Lyasra
  - Ismark is established as a **genuinely good leader under impossible pressure** — he knows his people's names, their losses, their worst impulses, and still chooses to believe in them
  - Lyasra's past is now partially on the table with Ismark — the exchange is brief but significant; he is one of the few characters she has spoken to at length
  - The **eastern defenses** are the party's assigned position for the night siege — the party has implicitly committed to holding them
  - The Svalich Woods threat profile is now established: druidic cult, werewolf pack, undead congregation, disorienting even in daylight

  

**Key NPCs developed:**

  

  - **Ismark** — Better characterized. Carries grief for his community without drowning in it. His aside about Ireena being able to best him with a rapier is a small but telling moment — he respects her rather than merely protecting her.

  

**Open threads:**

  

  - Who was the suspicious laborer, and why did he move away when the mob was discussed? Is he connected to the anti-Ireena faction? (Answer: He was related to the mob)
