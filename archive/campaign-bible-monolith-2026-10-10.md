# SECTION 2: TONE & STYLE GUIDE

## Campaign Atmosphere

This campaign runs a **gothic horror** register — psychologically rich, atmospherically dense, and thematically concerned with grief, captivity, identity, and the seductive nature of darkness. Barovia is not merely a backdrop; it is an active antagonist with its own moods and pressures.

  

Players will have privileged knowledge that they will need to choose to share or not share.

## Primary Narrative Voice: Darkest Dungeon Narrator Style

All scene narration and GM-facing prose should draw on the **Darkest Dungeon narrator** as a model:

  

  - **Victorian Gothic diction** — elevated, slightly archaic, never modern or casual
  - **Fatalism** — the environment and situation are described as almost inevitably oppressive; hope is fragile and hard-won
  - **Personified environments** — the village, the fog, the castle are described as *feeling* and *intending*, not merely existing
  - **Aphoristic rhythm** — sentences often land with the weight of a proverb; short declarative beats follow longer build-ups
  - **Never comedic or ironic** — the tone is sincere and relentless

  

*Example of correct register:* "The inn offers warmth, but not comfort. Its walls have absorbed too many desperate nights for that."

  

*Example of incorrect register:* "The tavern looks pretty sketchy. There's a creepy barkeep and some shady folks in the corner."

## Secondary Influences

  - **Dragnacarta's Curse of Strahd Reloaded** is the **secondary source** for all NPC characterization and motivations. It is used for inspiration and ideas
  - **MandyMod's** homebrewed content is also used as a secondary resource for additional idea generation
  - Character voice work (dialogue, letters, internal monologue) may draw on specific additional references noted in individual NPC entries below. If something is in any of the below sections, it is prioritized as the primary source for this campaign.

## Formatting Defaults

Unless instructed otherwise, all creative outputs should follow these conventions:

  

  - **Scene narrations:** Concise and short; read-aloud quality prose. Does not give directions / "control" players
  - **Dialogue:** In-character, no stage directions unless specifically requested
  - **Letters/journals:** Full literary voice matching the character's personality
  - **Design notes:** After every creative output, provide a brief paragraph of design notes explaining the key mechanical or thematic choices made — what pivot point was chosen, what was emphasized, and why
  - **Summarization of Sessions**: Do not embellish. Keep concise and to the main plot points and key characterizations.
  - **Multi-NPC or branching encounter scenes:** Use screenplay format (see Section 6 template) rather than prose narration. This is the default for any scene involving dialogue with conditional player responses, multiple NPCs in conversation, or a sequence with distinct narrative beats.
  - **Canonicity tagging (added 2026-08-30):** Content that originates from GM-to-GM worldbuilding/ideation sessions (as opposed to actual play) is tagged **\[IDEA — NONCANONICAL\]** wherever it appears in this document. Untagged content reflects either established published-module baseline or events that have actually occurred in a logged session. See Section 8 for the full maintenance rule.

### Madam Eva

**Roleplaying Information**  
***Resonance.*** Madam Eva should inspire discomfort with her intimate knowledge of the players' pasts, gratitude for her dedication to the players' journey, and reassurance with her confident predictions.

  

***Emotions.*** Madam Eva's most frequent emotions are amusement, solemnity, concern, and contemplation.

  

***Motivations.*** Madam Eva wants to see the land of Barovia healed and freed from Strahd's corruption.

  

***Inspirations.*** When playing Madam Eva, channel The Ancient One (Doctor Strange) and the Fates (Hercules).

  

**Character Information**

  

***Persona.*** To the world, Madam Eva appears as a wise, yet maddened crone who speaks in prophecies and riddles. To those she trusts, she appears as a kind, yet frustratingly opaque and stubborn old woman.

  

***Morale.*** In a fight, Madam Eva would calmly insist that her opponent cease their attacks, then—if her Vistani aides prove unable to stop her assailant—weaken them with the harm spell before demanding their surrender.

  

***Relationships.*** Madam Eva alone knows that she is an avatar of the Seeker.

  

**True nature \[IDEA — NONCANONICAL, GM-only — not to be revealed except through play\]:** Madam Eva is the Autumn Fane (the Mountain Fane; domain: the future), who survived Strahd's rise by going into hiding and merging/pact-binding with a mortal woman named **Katarina**. This is why Strahd, when seeking a Tarokka reading, specifically invokes "Madam Eva" rather than any other Fane name. Her pact's purpose, from Katarina's side, was to free her "brother" (Strahd) from the curse of immortality — i.e. release him from vampirism, not kill him. She is capable of restoring her full divinity right now but knows doing so would kill her while she's this weak alone — hence her long game of maneuvering others (Cassandra, Von Richten, Argonvost historically, the party) into doing the restoration work for her. She does **not** know her sister (the Spring Fane) is trapped in amber at the Amber Temple — she was occupied manifesting the Katarina bond when that happened and has no memory of it. She sees Cassandra as a potential vessel to help restore the Fanes, possibly a future high priestess or another avatar/conduit for the Mountain Fane — this is her hope, not a settled fact. She secretly seeded the idea of dissipating Amber Shards out of the Temple into Argonvost's mind during the Silver Age, letting him believe it was his own plan, because her real goal was extracting the Spring Fane piece by piece (see Section 9). Whether she and Argonvost had a personal history (courtly acquaintance, possibly an unconsummated romance) is an optional, undecided detail. She keeps a Roc as a familiar/mount, tied to a hinted (unreconciled) blood connection between Katarina and the Mardikov/von Zarovich bloodline (see Section 9, §10.2). If the party ever pursues retrieving the Heart of Darkness for Strahd in exchange for leaving Barovia, she will actively try to stop them (see Section 10).

  
  

### Strahd von Zarovich

**Status: \[IDEA — NONCANONICAL elaboration on a published-module character.\]** Baseline facts (he is Barovia's vampire lord, killed his brother Sergei, obsesses over Tatiana's reincarnations) are pre-existing module canon; everything below is homebrew psychology/goals work not yet revealed to players.

  

**Roleplaying Information**

  

***Resonance.*** Complicated, not cartoonishly hateable — except in one deliberate place: his pursuit of Tatiana/Irena, which should land as viscerally "icky"/predatory (a hunter enjoying the chase; "wooing" as part of the hunt), so players want to see him defeated on that front specifically even while finding him sympathetic elsewhere.

  

***Emotions.*** Egocentric, prideful, entitled — masked by a polite, cunning, "gentlemanly" facade that is explicitly a mask, not a genuine trait.

  

***Motivations.*** Two root motivations, from which everything else descends: (1) **mastery of his own fate/destiny** — refusal to be controlled by anyone, chiefly the Dark Powers; and (2) a genuine **love of entertainment/spectacle**. Goals downstream of these: revenge on those who've killed him across past cycles; obtaining more power; escaping Barovia; claiming Irena/Tatiana (belief that he owns her); building his spy network and military strength.

  

**Character Information**

  

***Persona.*** Does not actually control the mists himself — that's the Dark Powers'/Fanes' domain. The belief that he does is a deliberately maintained illusion, upheld by the Vistani (who serve his interests for freedom to travel) and by killing/compelling anyone who tries to leave alone before word gets out. Only a handful of people know the deeper truth (it's enforced by werewolves, not his personal magic): Stanimir and Madam Eva certainly; Arturi likely.

  

***Morale.*** Values entertainment and spectacle; prefers manipulation, coercion, and long games over blunt force where possible.

  

***Relationships.*** Views virtually everyone in Barovia (barring the Vistani, his "birds") as living in a cage, and believes what he could offer Tatiana/Irena — wealth, protection, status — is a bigger, more comfortable cage than anyone else in Barovia could give her. # SECTION 9: COSMOLOGY — THE FANES, STRAHD'S DEATHS, AND THE ORDER OF THE SILVER DRAGON

**\[IDEA — NONCANONICAL\] Everything in this section originates from Chris & Michelle's worldbuilding/ideation sessions, not from actual play.** It is GM-only background until something in a session reveals or confirms it. Sources: the two-part Fanes/Von Richten worldbuilding session, the *Recapping Session — First Death and Silver Dragon Order*, *Strahd's Motivations, Goals and Character Development Discussion*, and the *\[Untitled\] Strahd Characterization & Reward Items session*.

## 9.1 — The Fanes: core structure

The Fanes are nature goddesses, each tied to a season, a time-domain (past/present/future), a worshipping population, and (for the worshipped three) an animal:

  

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Fane** | **Season** | **Time domain** | **Worshippers** | **Animal** | **Status** |
| **Spring** | Spring | Past | Swamp folk | Spider | Captured in amber by Baba Lysaga |
| **Summer** | Summer | Present | Forest folk | Wolf | Killed by Strahd; becomes the Gulthias Tree |
| **Autumn** | Autumn | Future | Mountain folk | Raven | Survives as **Madam Eva** |
| **Winter** | Winter | Death | None (unworshipped) | Unassigned/TBD | Corrupted; joined the Dark Powers/Mother Night; became the mists — **tentatively identified as Void Child/Khirad (REVISION, unresolved — see Section 4)** |

  

**Primordial beasts (added 2026-09-24, ideation):** each Fane also has a sentient **primordial beast** — Winter: **the Winter Worm** (Lake Zarovich); Spring: a great spider (Baba Lysaga's creeping hut floated as an automaton imitation of it); Summer: undecided; Autumn: **the Roc of Mount Gakis**. This effectively fills the Winter Fane's "Unassigned/TBD" animal slot with the worm, though the GMs framed it as a beast rather than a symbol. Full detail in **Section 15.1**.

## 9.2 — Founding tragedy ("Rise of Strahd")

  - The forest-fane druids invited Strahd to a ceremony honoring the Summer Fane, partly to curry favor with him ("we will be part of your kingdom"). Strahd used the occasion to kill the Summer Fane, whom he perceived as arrogant/overconfident in her own strength. Her spilled blood soaked into what had been a healthy, vibrant tree — creating the corrupted **Gulthias Tree** and, from that same bloodshed, **the birth of Barovia's werewolves**. The Summer Fane and her people are conceived as **Fey creatures** (dryad-type), not human.
  - The Spring Fane (swamp) held all knowledge of the past and ran communal memory-baptisms, but had no ability to see the future and so never foresaw Strahd's coming. **Baba Lysaga** — a former priestess of the Spring Fane — eventually captures the Spring Fane in amber, motivated by love for Strahd and a desire to absorb the Fane's memory-collecting power to become, in her mind, Strahd's "true mother." Strahd, unable to tolerate her instability, has Rahadin exile her to the ruins of Berez (not yet ruins at that point).
  - Floated as a possible pun/etymology for "Amber Temple" (Baba Lysaga's amber-based imprisonment) — needs reconciling with the Temple's established deeper history as an ancient magical nexus predating her. The Amber Temple is not the *birth* of the Dark Powers, but rather an ancient magical "ley line" convergence that predates them, which the Dark Powers later co-opted. This is where Strahd went to obtain his vampirism.
  - The Winter Fane, having never been worshipped, is drawn to **Mother Night** and the Dark Powers, who whisper to her; in exchange she becomes the entity that powers the mists that seal Barovia.
  - The Mountain Fane (Autumn/future) survives by going into hiding and merging/pact-binding with a mortal woman named **Katarina** to become **Madam Eva** (full detail under Madam Eva's NPC entry, Section 4). Madam Eva does not know her sister (the Spring Fane) is trapped in amber — she was occupied manifesting the Katarina bond when that happened and has no memory of it.

## 9.3 — Restoration conditions

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Fane** | **Condition to restore** |
| Spring (swamp) | Freed from Baba Lysaga's amber imprisonment |
| Summer (forest) | Gulthias Tree healed/purified |
| Autumn (mountain) | Already effectively "restored" as Madam Eva — her win condition is to **survive and stay hidden** until the others are freed |
| Winter | Friendship / no longer alone |

  

  - Madam Eva could restore her own full divinity now, but knows doing so would kill her while she's this weak alone — hence her long game of manipulating others (Cassandra, Von Richten, Argonvost historically, the party) into doing the restoration work for her.
  - Von Richten's undead-caching activity is tied to Madam Eva — she covertly helped implant/encourage his obsession with Summer Fane lore as one of her ongoing (morally grim) attempts to make progress restoring the Summer Fane. Offered as her rationale for tolerating him despite his hatred of the Vistani.
  - Cassandra's role: Madam Eva sees her (via the Tarokka gift/prophetic visions) as a potential vessel to help restore the Fanes, possibly a future high priestess or another avatar/conduit for the Mountain Fane — this is Eva's hope, not a fixed fact.

## 9.4 — Timeline naming conventions

  - **Age of Fanes** — pre-Ravenloft period when all three worshipped Fanes existed and were venerated.
  - **Rise of Strahd** — the founding tragedy: Strahd's ascension, the killing of the Summer Fane, Baba Lysaga's capture of the Spring Fane and exile, the Winter Fane's corruption.

## 9.5 — Strahd's deaths & the Order of the Silver Dragon

**REVISION note:** an earlier pass of this timeline had the Order of the Silver Dragon founded "at the same time as Markovia is fighting Strahd." The *Recapping Session* revises this: **Lord Argonvost** (a dragon in disguise as a nobleman) and his fortified holt predate Strahd's rule entirely, guarding the Amber Temple as "a repository of evil power" long before Strahd's rise — not to fight Strahd, and without any awareness the Fanes were sealed inside. Markovia's role isn't founding the order; it's **converting** an already-existing, neutral Argonvost into an active ally by convincing him appeasement doesn't guarantee the Temple's safety. (Ironic detail: by preventing anything leaving the Temple, Argonvost's vigilance inadvertently made his order the Spring Fane's jailer too, without knowing she was there.)

  

1.  **Andrel's Reprieve — Strahd's 1st vampire death.** Saint Andrew kills Strahd using personal knowledge of his weaknesses (they knew each other before). Andrew's bones remain in Vallaki, which becomes important later. Ushers in a period of peace; likely when Vallaki grows into Barovia's largest settlement. Mists have not yet rolled in.
2.  **Markovia's Reprieve — Strahd's 2nd vampire death.** Saint Markovia (Tatiana's 2nd major reincarnation — see §9.6) convinces Argonvost to act; he arms her with the **Sun Sword** (kept inside the Amber Temple), after she proves herself worthy. She kills Strahd. An uneasy "**weary peace**" follows — unlike Andrew's death, people know he could return, they just don't know how or why. Her remains are later brought to Krezk, which protects that location from Strahd's future revenge.
3.  **Argonvost & the Silver Dragon Knights are slaughtered** when Strahd returns (a "2.5," not a true death). Because Argonvost's remains are kept as a trophy in Castle Ravenloft rather than consecrated ground, Strahd is able to target and destroy the order directly. Reysatra and Argonvost survive the Markovia-era fight but are killed in this later reprisal.
      
      - **The Silver Age (interim era):** a survived-and-recovering Argonvost issues a call to arms that eventually recruits Reysatra. He begins quietly dissipating Amber Shards out of the Temple to dilute its concentration of dark power — secretly Madam Eva's idea, which she lets him believe is his own, since her real goal is extracting the Spring Fane piece by piece. Whether Argonvost and Katarina/Madam Eva had a personal history (courtly acquaintance, possibly an unconsummated romance) is optional/undecided.
      - **"Soul Trap Discovery" (unresolved):** during this era, Barovia's people are proposed to begin realizing souls of the dead aren't properly departing the demiplane. The original discovery method (tied to consecrated bone-sites) was self-identified as chronologically inconsistent and left open — "further research of the bones" TBD.
      - **Berez's ruin:** explicitly NOT Argonvost/Strahd razing Berez in revenge on Markovia (rejected). Instead: **Baba Lysaga performs a ritual sacrifice, using Berez as fuel to resurrect Strahd** after Markovia's kill, further depleting the Spring Fane's power she already holds captive — explaining both her present weakness and Berez's eventual ruin. (Reference-only, not adopted: raw-module Marina/Berez backstory, and a Dragnacarta/MandyMod alternative involving Strahd's bride Patrona originating from Berez — neither is canon.)
      - Post-resurrection Strahd (this era) becomes notably more cunning: kills Argonvost and destroys the order; destroys historical knowledge of his mortal/early-vampire self (this is why the Tome of Strahd, Section 11, survived only by being hidden somewhere even he couldn't find it); builds his spy network; begins isolating settlements; starts subjugating Barovia's werewolves into service rather than them being independently aligned with him.
4.  **Lugdana's era — Strahd's temporary stasis (not a true death).** Ismark the Great, Lugdana, and the Mordakov/Mardikov family band together — aided by werewolf knowledge and possibly the wereravens — to bind Strahd in stasis, since by this point it's understood a true kill is impossible while the Fanes remain unrestored. Madam Eva may have given them the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind as a stopgap, knowing they couldn't win outright. **Deliberate timeline-compression decision:** the gap between Strahd's second return and this binding is kept short (100–200 years, leaning short) so a few very long-lived witnesses (chiefly Reysatra) can still personally remember this era, and so this Strahd reads rawer/more reckless than the patient, learned Strahd the party faces today — the March of the Dead is a long parade of failed champions, Lugdana's expedition merely the last of them, each failure sharpening Strahd's tactics. Lugdana dies to werewolves; Ismark the Great dies of natural causes while laying the foundations of the village of Barovia.
5.  **Present day.** Strahd returns from stasis and retaliates against the Mordakov family's ancestors, Doru, and the village — the villagers' current persecution/hero-worship dynamic stems from Doru and Kolyan Indirovich taking the blame for their ancestors' role in the historical binding.

  

**Key clarification:** the Mordakov (Mardikov) family are the last surviving lineage with direct, deliberate historical knowledge of this entire cycle — hereditary bookkeepers/archivists distinct from the villagers' incomplete "hero worship" version. Kolyan may have left hidden records with them. Meant to be a discoverable thread for the party, not disclosed directly.

  

**Discarded/softened idea:** whether the Mordakov family or Madam Eva "betrayed" earlier heroes by sending them to die as fodder (vs. informed volunteers) was reframed — the heroes weren't told upfront that killing Strahd outright was impossible without the Fanes restored, but this stays a bittersweet realization-in-hindsight, not a cynical betrayal, per your stated preference for hope over pure tragedy.

  

**Explicitly rejected direction:** making Strahd's central motivation simply "win Tatiana's true love, full stop" — felt too flat ("achy"). Preferred alternative: Strahd may believe (mistakenly or not) that Tatiana/her reincarnations literally **are** the Autumn Fane, since she represents the future and he's always sought his future through her — reframing pursuit of her as instrumental (a possible path out of the mists) rather than pure romantic obsession.

  

**Shelved:** a folk-tale idea — a superhumanly powerful "goat," the **Terror of Mount Ballanok**, tying Strahd thematically to the land itself. Not developed further.

# SECTION 11: THE TOME OF STRAHD — QUESTLINE

**\[IDEA — NONCANONICAL\]** Source: *Von Richten's Darkening Path and Faction Power Struggles*.

  

An artifact resembling Strahd's own diary/journal — giving deep, humanizing backstory, and potentially his own research notes on communing with/summoning the Dark Powers. A fan-transcribed excerpt opens in Strahd's own voice: *"I am the ancient. I am the land… I was the warrior… good and just… my army settled in the valley of Barovia… I called for my family… my brother Sergei…"* — intended as a base text to expand with campaign-specific additions (e.g., his Amber Temple research). **Decided:** Strahd would not keep a physical map of Castle Ravenloft itself lying around in his own home — too great a personal security risk.

  

**In-world terminology:** "Dark Powers" is OOC/GM-facing shorthand only, since it sounds too overtly evil to say diegetically. In-world, NPCs (particularly any priest/priestess of this force) should refer to it as **"Mother Night."**

  

**Where the Tome physically hides:** the Volokovich mansion's enchanted "Spirit Mirror" (per raw material: detectable conjuration aura, unused for generations, identify reveals it's bound to a summonable assassin's ghost via the rhyme *"Magic mirror on the wall, summon forth your shade…"*). New idea: this mirror was a gift from Saint Andrew to the Volokovich family, and doubles as the hidden vessel for the Tome — Andrew used the intimate personal knowledge recorded there to defeat Strahd the first time, and hid it this way to preserve it. # SECTION 15: SESSION 10 PREP — LAKE ZAROVICH, THE WINTER WORM, THE WITHERING SICKNESS & THE VALLAKI APPROACH

## 15.2 — The Winter Worm & the Heart of Darkness

The two framings are compatible — a weakening seal is when the worm stirs enough to yawn.
  - **Why it's stirring now:** something Strahd has done since his reawakening (~one month ago in-world) — his efforts to find/reach the Winter Fane, and specifically the long-running poisoning campaign (15.4) — is weakening the seal and waking the worm. **The Void Child's proximity alone would not wake anything**; only a real triggering event (e.g. a Winter-Fane-linked object actually going into the water) would.
  - **The worm is lonely,** in keeping with the Winter Fane's restoration condition ("friendship / no longer alone," Section 9.3). In the late stages of infection its parasites may transmit that loneliness to their hosts (15.3).
  - **Heart of Darkness — unresolved details:**
      - 2026-09-15: Michelle suggested the Heart is some manifestation of power **held within the worm**. 2026-09-19: the working version is that it's a **relic at the lake bottom guarded by the worm**, and Strahd must **kill the worm** to take it. Not reconciled — both are live.
      - Is it one stone or several? Floated: one great stone that, if broken, "comets out into the sky" and scatters like seeds for the party to hunt down; or it's the strongest of the Fanes' powers; or it's the piece needed to bring the others together; or it splits into three. **Undecided.**

## 15.3 — The Withering Sickness (parasites + poison)

  - **Parasites:** small worm-like parasites are pieces sloughing off the waking Winter Worm. They were dormant until Strahd's return and have only appeared in roughly the last month. Naming: the GMs settled on **"the Winter Worm" for the single giant beast and just "parasites" for the small ones**. ("Winter worms" for the parasites and "great worm" for the beast were both considered and dropped. The spelling "wyrm" was also floated.)
  - **Transmission is by drinking the water, not eating the fish.** Fish get cooked; untreated lake water doesn't. **Vallaki draws from wells, which is why the town itself is largely safe;** the Barovian refugees camped outside drink straight from Lake Zarovich.
  - **Water flow (geography, important):** water runs **Luna Lake → Luna River → Lake Zarovich → onward toward Mount Baratok.** Parasites spread downstream of the lake, away from the upstream Vistani camp. Strahd's poison (15.4) enters **upstream**, so it reaches everyone downstream of its source.
  - **Who is sick with what:**
      - **Barovian refugees:** poison **and** parasites — the worst off.
      - **Vistani camp near Vallaki:** poison only (upstream of the parasites). They noticed they were getting sick, switched to *purify food and drink*, and improved, but that spell only scales to a small group. They don't blame the water. They blame the Barovians: "ever since they started storming Vallaki they have brought disease and famine." They have also noticed animals dying.
      - **Vallakians:** mostly protected by the wells. (Earlier idea: poorer fish-eating commoners get sick more than the wealthy. Superseded by the wells/drinking framing but compatible with it.)
  - **Symptoms ("withering sickness"):** appetite and satiety changes, constant hunger and thirst, anemia, bruxism, digestive trouble, fatigue, joint and muscle pain, skin issues, sleep problems, swelling, and progressive malnourishment/wasting. It also brings **depression/hopelessness**, fitting the Winter Fane's theme. People are dying in droves. **Full life-cycle stages are still TBD** — the plan is to base them on real parasitism.
  - **Late-stage hive-mind (floated):** advanced parasites may relay the worm's feelings (its loneliness) to their hosts and compel them to bring things (people) to the lake (see Arabelle, 15.8).
  - **Rejected/narrowed:** an earlier (2026-09-15) idea that infection targets only people with a specific trait (hopelessness) was floated. It was noted that this would infect a lot of Barovians. It could still explain why Vargas insists everyone be happy — "unhappy people fall into despair and become prone to infection." The later drinking-water model replaced it as the mechanism, though despair remains thematically tied in.
  - **Lyasra's *purify food and drink* is now story-relevant** (Michelle's observation).
  - **Lycanthropy cut from the refugee camp for scope:** some refugees have been bitten on the road, but none of the bitten remain in the camp now (Vargas has them lynched on sight: "Werewolves are the servants of Strahd, and we do not stand Strahd in this house"). The refugees still report werewolf attacks on the road and may ask the party for protection.

## 15.4 — Strahd's Machination: Poisoning the Winter Worm

**This is the "action Strahd is taking" the GMs had been missing.**

  - **Goal:** obtain the Heart of Darkness. Strahd can't enter running water, and the worm is too dangerous to fight awake, so he is **poisoning it to death slowly** and making the lake uninhabitable for it.
  - **Who runs it:** **Ludmilla** (Strahd's first/eldest bride, Ludmilla Vilisevic — Section 4; canonically the vampire the party drove off in Session 9) has made this her sole long-term task **since Lugdana's era** (Strahd saw that the Heart might be an alternative power source after that defeat). ## 16.3 — Getting into Vallaki

  - **What the party wants:** get into Vargas's mansion to look for Stella. They've also talked about overthrowing or killing Vargas. GM questions to answer: what happened to Stella? How do they find the mansion? How did the diary reach Morgantha (less important)?
  - **The walls:** Vallaki is walled on all sides, with portcullised gates and no port (Lake Zarovich is a walk away). Climbing is very hard. Roughly a mile long. The Vistani camp sits near the most natural gate; the refugee camp is outside the gates, about **15 minutes** from where the party is.

## 16.4 — The night: rest or infiltrate

The party chooses to **rest** at the Vistani camp or **infiltrate** Vallaki tonight.

**If they rest — the werewolf raid on the refugee camp (~10 PM).**

  - **Replaces the routine bat-and-wolf harassment** (the refugees already report nightly bats and wolves; Reyna has heard about it). This one is out of the ordinary: **Kiril's werewolves come to steal children** for his fight pit.
  - **Werewolf background (RAW, adopted):**
      - The pack's leader is **Kiril Stoyanovich**. **Emil Toranescu** challenged his practice of arming kidnapped children to fight to the death, with the survivor turned into a werewolf.
      - Kiril returned with dire wolves loyal to Strahd. Emil was taken to **Castle Ravenloft**, where he's a prisoner in the dungeon (the transcript is unclear whether he's rescuable; RAW allows it).
      - Emil's mate **Zuleika** is confined to the den.
      - **The Holy Symbol of Ravenkind is in the werewolf den** — a strong hook pulling the party there (consistent with the Luna Lake location).
  - **The encounter:**
      - Combat objective: **stop the wolves carrying off children.** However many children are taken become fighters in Kiril's ring.
      - Larger than Dragnacarta's road version (two werewolves and three wolves): more werewolves moving through the camp.
      - Kiril probably sends a trusted lieutenant rather than coming himself — the GMs don't want him dying here. A Dragnacarta "first Kiril encounter" is worth checking.
      - Michelle floated an OC sympathetic to Emil who wants to turn some of the children so they have a chance of retaking the pack. Chris is open to it but prefers Kiril raiding for the strongest children himself. Undecided.
  - **Lycanthropy risk:** a PC bitten by a werewolf can be cursed with **evil lycanthropy** — unlike Jacob's, they begin to feel the hunger. Curing it requires a restoration spell, which **the Deva at Krezk** can provide — a deliberate time pressure.
  - **Rest cost:** the party can still long rest after the fight, but takes **one level of exhaustion** (disadvantage on ability checks). The GMs agreed losing the rest entirely was too harsh; the principle is that helping people should cost something.
  - **Izek's response:** holds the gate (see his entry).
  - **The bones:** St. Andral's bones are stolen on the evening after the rest (wording in the transcript is ambiguous; the theft happens around this rest).
  - **Considered and dropped as the night's attack:**
      - an assassination attempt on Irena by Anastrasya;
      - werewolves attacking Irena to win Strahd's favor (as the daughter of the Burgomaster whose town Strahd has been attacking).

**If they infiltrate tonight:**

  - Izek's patrol and possibly the Blinsky's satchel sighting.
  - Ernst shadowing Izek, opening the Watcher thread.
  - **The refugee camp loses whatever children are left** — the raid happens without the party.
