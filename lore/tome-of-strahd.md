# SECTION 11: THE TOME OF STRAHD — QUESTLINE

**\[IDEA — NONCANONICAL\]** Source: *Von Richten's Darkening Path and Faction Power Struggles*.

  

An artifact resembling Strahd's own diary/journal — giving deep, humanizing backstory, and potentially his own research notes on communing with/summoning the Dark Powers. A fan-transcribed excerpt opens in Strahd's own voice: *"I am the ancient. I am the land… I was the warrior… good and just… my army settled in the valley of Barovia… I called for my family… my brother Sergei…"* — intended as a base text to expand with campaign-specific additions (e.g., his Amber Temple research). **Decided:** Strahd would not keep a physical map of Castle Ravenloft itself lying around in his own home — too great a personal security risk.

  

**In-world terminology:** "Dark Powers" is OOC/GM-facing shorthand only, since it sounds too overtly evil to say diegetically. In-world, NPCs (particularly any priest/priestess of this force) should refer to it as **"Mother Night."**

  

**Where the Tome physically hides:** the Volokovich mansion's enchanted "Spirit Mirror" (per raw material: detectable conjuration aura, unused for generations, identify reveals it's bound to a summonable assassin's ghost via the rhyme *"Magic mirror on the wall, summon forth your shade…"*). New idea: this mirror was a gift from Saint Andrew to the Volokovich family, and doubles as the hidden vessel for the Tome — Andrew used the intimate personal knowledge recorded there to defeat Strahd the first time, and hid it this way to preserve it. Styled explicitly as a Sorcerer's-Stone/Mirror-of-Erised analog. Two spells cast in sequence (detect magic, then identify — ritually cast) are needed to fully uncover it, deliberately gating discovery. This ties the Volokovich family's self-styled claim to be Barovia's most noble, most anti-Strahd house (via their ancestral bond with Saint Andrew) to the tome's literal physical location.

  

**REVISION [IDEA — NONCANONICAL], 2026-09-19:** the Spirit Mirror's Erised-style effect is changed from showing **what you most desire** to showing **the future** (the Tome is "of the past"; the reflection shows what's coming). This is now the preferred version. It explains Stella's madness (she saw her own parasite-riddled future — see her Section 4 entry) and Victor's cruelty (he sees himself becoming his father).

  

**Multiple parallel NPC leads toward the Tome** (intentionally redundant/branching, so whichever thread the players pick up still works — see full NPC entries in Section 4):

  

  - **Casimir Velikov** — leads toward Petrina's spirit (the "lost soul" from the Nine-of-Glyphs clue), who can point either directly toward knowledge of the Tome or toward Casimir himself, who then points toward the Volokovich mansion.
  - **Lady Fiona Watcher / Stella** — Stella's madness is an alternate "lost soul" lead, requiring the party to parse sense out of her ravings. Ties less cleanly into the wider Vallaki plot than the Casimir/Petrina branch.
  - **Victor Volokovich** — his established vanity (staring into ornate mirrors) organically points players toward the mirror. **Explicitly rejected:** making the mirror an overtly sentient, "basically Tom Riddle" manipulator whispering to him — too on-the-nose.

  

**Intended tone:** a possible heist-style approach to the mansion — multiple valid entry methods (invited in, sneaking in during a festival, getting arrested, killing everyone and searching) — with Isaac "Izek" Strazani and roughly 36 retained mansion guards (per raw material) as the defensive complication.
